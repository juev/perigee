# Research

Research notes for questions that may or may not lead to changes in Perigee. Nothing here is a specification or a commitment to build a feature: decisions are taken separately, by the owner, and recorded in issues and in [docs/specs](../specs).

## Reading with low vision

Issue [#144](https://github.com/juev/perigee/issues/144), 2026-11-02, at commit `6bce6fa` (v0.1.0-rc11).

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [low-vision-reading.md](low-vision-reading.md) | The review: why size alone is not enough on a small screen, what the evidence supports and does not, Android and W3C criteria, gaps, and decisions left open |
| [low-vision-baseline.md](low-vision-baseline.md) | What Perigee already does, read from the source at the baseline commit |
| [low-vision-source-notes.md](low-vision-source-notes.md) | The 28 works read: title, link, date where verified, population and task, quotes, limits |
| [low-vision-candidates.csv](low-vision-candidates.csv) | URLs found while searching; a discovery log, not a list of read or validated studies |

Two kinds of evidence are kept apart. **Source evidence** is what the code and the specs say, linked to lines at a pinned commit. **Device evidence** would be what the app does on a phone, with TalkBack, magnification or a reader; none has been collected for these notes. Clinical and platform sources are external evidence, each with the limits given in the source notes. None of these files diagnoses anyone or selects a prototype or a trial.
